📘 Chapter 1: Accounting Principles & Financial Reporting

Accounting principles are accepted rules and practices used to determine:

  • Which assets and liabilities are recorded
  • How assets and liabilities are valued
  • What income and expenses are recognised
  • How financial statements are prepared

🧠 Memory Aid

GHACM

G = Going Concern
H = Historical Cost
A = Accrual Basis
C = Consistency
M = Materiality

🏢 Going Concern

Business is expected to continue operating in the foreseeable future.

Assets should not be valued at break-up value.

Example

Machine Cost = $60,000

Accumulated Depreciation = $10,000

Carrying Value = $50,000

✅ Use $50,000

❌ Do not use scrap value of $8,000

📜 Historical Cost

Assets are recorded at original purchase cost.

Market value changes are ignored.

Example

Land purchased = $50,000

Current market value = $100,000

✅ Financial Statements = $50,000

⏳ Accrual Basis

Income is recognised when earned.

Expenses are recognised when incurred.

Not based on cash movement.

Revenue Earned → Record Revenue

Expense Incurred → Record Expense

🔄 Consistency

Similar items receive similar treatment.

Methods should remain consistent from year to year.

FIFO This Year
↓
FIFO Next Year

Straight Line Depreciation
↓
Continue Using It

📌 Materiality

Information is material if it affects decisions.

Error = $900

Sales = $10,000 ✅ Material

Sales = $10 million ❌ May be immaterial

📚 Other Accounting Principles

Principle Meaning
Business Entity Business separate from owner
Double Entry Every transaction has two effects
Money Measurement Only items measurable in money are recorded
Separate Valuation Assets and liabilities valued individually

🌍 Accounting Standards

Accounting standards ensure financial statements present a:

  • True and Fair View
  • Consistency
  • Comparability
  • Reliability
✅ Major International Standards

IFRS = International Financial Reporting Standards
IAS = International Accounting Standards

Accounting Policy Accounting Estimate

Depreciate PPE Straight Line 10%

💡 Qualitative Characteristics

Fundamental Characteristics

✅ Relevance

Capable of influencing decisions.

Includes:

  • Predictive Value
  • Confirmatory Value

✅ Faithful Representation

  • Complete
  • Neutral
  • Free From Error

Enhancing Characteristics

Characteristic Meaning
Comparability Compare different periods and entities
Verifiability Can be checked and verified
Timeliness Available in time for decisions
Understandability Easy for users to understand

⚠ Common Exam Mistakes

Historical Cost ≠ Market Value
Trial Balance can balance and still contain errors
Accrual Basis ≠ Cash Basis

📒 Chapter 2: Control Accounts & Correction of Errors

Control Accounts help businesses check accuracy, locate errors quickly, and improve internal controls.

🧠 Memory Aid

Control Account = Total
Personal Account = Individual
Reconcile = Compare
Suspense = Temporary

📊 Control Accounts

📘 Control Account

Summary account in the General Ledger.

Examples:

  • Receivables Control Account
  • Payables Control Account
  • Inventory Control Account
  • Wages Control Account

👤 Personal Account

Records transactions of one customer or supplier.

Not part of the Double Entry System.

✅ Key Rule

Receivables Control Account

=

Total Individual Customer Balances

🎯 Why Use Control Accounts?

🔍 Accuracy Check

Detect omission and transposition errors.

🛡 Internal Control

Different staff can maintain different records.

⚡ Faster Detection

Errors can be found earlier through reconciliation.

📚 Books of Prime Entry

Book Records
Sales Day Book Sales Invoices
Sales Return Day Book Credit Notes Sent
Purchase Day Book Purchase Invoices
Purchase Returns Day Book Credit Notes Received
Cash Received Day Book Cash Received
Cash Payments Day Book Cash Payments
Petty Cash Book Small Cash Transactions
Journal Adjustments

🔄 Reconciliation

Control accounts should be reconciled at least monthly.

✅ Reconciliation compares control account balances with personal account balances.

Common Causes of Differences

  • Transposition Errors
  • Incorrect Totals
  • Incorrect Extraction of Balances
  • Wrong Side Entries
  • Contra Entries Missing
  • Discounts Not Recorded
  • Credit Notes Treated as Invoices

🚨 Types of Errors

🔢 Transposition Error

523 recorded as 532

❌ Omission Error

Transaction not recorded.

📋 Principle Error

Wrong category.

Vehicle → Expense instead of Asset

🎯 Commission Error

Correct category Wrong account.

⚖ Compensating Error

Two errors cancel each other.

✍ Correcting Errors

✅ Trial Balance Balances

Use Journal Entry

Dr Correct Account
Cr Correct Account

❌ Trial Balance Doesn't Balance

Use Suspense Account

⚠ Suspense Account

Temporary account used when:

  • Debits ≠ Credits
  • Destination account unknown
Suspense Account MUST be cleared before Financial Statements are prepared.

📝 Worked Example

Customer pays $460. Bookkeeper debits Trade Receivable instead of crediting it.

Dr Suspense $920
Cr Trade Receivable $920

Because:

  • Remove wrong debit = $460
  • Add correct credit = $460
  • Total correction = $920

⚠ Exam Traps

Trial Balance Balances ≠ Accounts Correct

Errors of Principle may still exist.

Errors of Omission may still exist.

Compensating Errors may still exist.

📘 Chapter 3: Accruals & Prepayments

Accruals and prepayments ensure income and expenses are recorded in the correct accounting period.

🧠 Memory Aid

Accrual = Owe Money
Prepayment = Already Paid
Accrued Income = Earned Not Received
Deferred Income = Received Not Earned

⏳ Accrual Basis

💰 Income

Recognised when earned.

Not when cash is received.

💸 Expenses

Recognised when incurred.

Not when paid.

🎯 Matching Principle

Expenses must be matched against the revenue they generate.

📊 The Four Timing Adjustments

Item Meaning Classification
Accrued Expense Expense incurred but unpaid Current Liability
Prepaid Expense Expense paid for future period Current Asset
Accrued Income Income earned but not received Asset
Deferred Income Cash received before earning income Liability

📌 Accrued Expenses

Expense incurred but not yet paid.

✅ Reported as Current Liability
Dr Expense
Cr Accrual
Example

Electricity Expense Outstanding = $4,327.68

Dr Electricity Expense $4,327.68

Cr Accruals $4,327.68

💳 Prepaid Expenses

Expense already paid but relates to a future accounting period.

✅ Reported as Current Asset
Dr Prepayment
Cr Expense
Example

Rent Paid = $25,000

Future Period = $5,000

Expense = $20,000

📈 Accrued Income

Income earned but cash not yet received.

✅ Recorded as Asset

Consulting Service Completed = $5,000

Invoice sent next month.

Income must still be recognised.

📉 Deferred Income

Cash received before income is earned.

✅ Recorded as Liability

12-Month Magazine Subscription

Cash received in January.

Revenue recognised monthly.

💡 Worked Example

Rent = $12,000 per year

Business operated for 8 months

Cash Paid = $6,000

  • Rent Expense = $8,000
  • Cash Paid = $6,000
  • Accrued Rent = $2,000

⚠ Exam Traps

Cash Paid ≠ Expense

Cash Received ≠ Revenue

Accrual = Liability

Prepayment = Asset

📖 Chapter 3 Glossary

Term Meaning

Matching Principle Match expenses to related revenue.
Accrued Expense Expense incurred but unpaid.
Prepaid Expense Expense paid in advance.
Accrued Income Income earned but not received.

SOFP Statement of Financial Position.
SOPL Statement of Profit or Loss.

📘 Chapter 4: Irrecoverable Debts, Receivables Allowance & Provisions

Not all customers pay their debts. Businesses must account for debts that will not be collected and estimate future losses.

🧠 Memory Aid

Gone = Write Off
Might Not Pay = Allowance
Uncertain Liability = Provision

📄 Receivables

💰 Trade Receivables

Amounts owed by customers for credit sales.

📋 Non-Trade Receivables

Amounts owed for non-trading reasons.

Example: Loan to Employees

❌ Irrecoverable Debts

A specific debt that is not expected to be paid.

Common Reasons

  • Customer Bankruptcy
  • Customer Closure
  • Dishonesty
  • Financial Difficulty
Dr Receivables Expense
Cr Trade Receivable

🧾 VAT / Sales Tax Relief

When an irrecoverable debt includes VAT, the business may receive tax relief.

Debt = $3,800
VAT Portion = $633.33
Net Debt = $3,166.67
Dr VAT Account $633.33
Cr Irrecoverable Debts Expense $633.33

🔄 Recovery of Irrecoverable Debt

Sometimes a debt written off is later recovered.

✅ Recovered Before Year End

Dr Receivables Control
Cr Receivables Expense

✅ Recovered After Year End

Dr Bank
Cr Sundry Income

📊 Receivables Allowance

Allowance recognises that some debts may not be collected in the future.

❌ Direct Write-off

Evidence customer will NOT pay.

⚠ Allowance

Evidence customer MIGHT not pay.

Estimate future losses.

📚 Types of Allowance

Type Meaning
Specific Allowance Applies to a specific doubtful customer
General Allowance Based on past business experience

✍ Creating an Allowance

Dr Receivables Expense
Cr Allowance for Receivables
Receivables = $28,000
Allowance Rate = 1%
Allowance = $280

🔄 Movement in Allowance

Situation Entry
Opening < Closing Dr Expense / Cr Allowance
Opening > Closing Dr Allowance / Cr Expense

⚖ Provisions

A provision is a liability of uncertain timing or amount.

✅ IAS 37 Conditions
  • Present Obligation
  • Probable Economic Outflow
  • Reliable Estimate Exists

📒 Provision Accounting

When Provision Created

Dr Expense
Cr Provision

When Paid Later

Dr Provision
Cr Bank

⚠ Exam Traps

Irrecoverable Debt ≠ Allowance

Allowance = Estimate

Provision = Liability

Suspense Account ≠ Provision

Chapter 1

What are Accounting Principles?
Accepted rules and practices used to prepare financial statements.
What does GHACM stand for?
Going Concern, Historical Cost, Accrual Basis, Consistency, Materiality.
What is Going Concern?
The business is expected to continue operating in the foreseeable future.
Under Going Concern, are assets valued at break-up value?
No. Assets are valued based on continued use, not liquidation value.
What is Historical Cost?
Assets are recorded at their original purchase price.
Does Historical Cost use market value?
No. It uses the original purchase cost.
What is Accrual Basis?
Income is recognised when earned and expenses when incurred.
Accrual Basis focuses on cash movement?
No. It focuses on when income is earned and expenses incurred.
What is Consistency?
Applying the same accounting treatment from one period to another.
Consistency supports which characteristic?
Comparability.
What is Materiality?
Information that could influence users’ economic decisions.
Can the same error be material for one company but immaterial for another?
Yes. Materiality depends on size and circumstances.
What is the Business Entity Principle?
The business is treated separately from its owner.
What is Double Entry Bookkeeping?
Every transaction has two effects: a debit and a credit.
What is Money Measurement Principle?
Only items measurable in monetary terms are recorded.
What is Separate Valuation Principle?
Each asset or liability component is valued separately.
What are Accounting Standards?
Rules and guidelines governing preparation and presentation of financial statements.
